Chapter 1

A battery is a water tank

Energy is the water in the tank, power is how fast it flows out, and voltage is the pressure. Move the slider to change the workload and watch what happens. The numbers come from your personal laptop.

Battery %100%
Energy left38.9 Wh

Time left = energy ÷ power. That's how Windows guesses, which is why the guess jumps whenever power jumps. The pressure needle is a simplified picture of voltage sag.

      Chapter 6
      
    What the data showed
    

      
    
        
    01The gauge froze at 7% for 31 minutes
    
        
    Remaining energy stuck at exactly 2,710 mWh from 4:43 to 5:11 am while the laptop kept drawing about 4.7 W. Roughly 2.4 Wh went unreported, then the gauge recalibrated and full-charge capacity dropped 4.4%.
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    02Windows' time-left guess swung by up to 57%
    
        
    It divides energy left by the last few minutes of power use, so short spikes crash the guess and quiet stretches inflate it. It settles down below about 25%.
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    03Heavier work, steeper drain, lower voltage
    
        
    
          
    Light use6.5 W · 18%/h
    
          
    VM running8.4 W · 25%/h
    
          
    VM + HD video12.1 W · 36%/h
    
        
    
        
    
        
    CPU stayed around 4–7% the whole time. Video runs on dedicated video-decoding hardware, so CPU alone doesn't explain power use.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    04Two laptops, two charging styles
    
        
    The personal laptop charges in stages and slows down near full, then keeps charging 26 minutes after it already shows 100%. The work laptop runs a flat ~26 W right up to full and stops.

    Work laptop37.7 of 50.0 Wh · 75% health · 38 cycles

    06The "overnight drain" mystery, solved
    
        
    The work laptop kept dying overnight. A 2.5-hour sleep test lost only 4%, so sleep wasn't the problem. Step through Oct 8 to see what was.
    
        
    
          
          
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    07Two bugs in my own tools
    
        
    
          
    Logger: Windows reports "no estimate" as 4,294,967,295 seconds (136 years). The first version wrote it as a real number. Fixed, with a test.
    
          
    Analysis: the estimate check assumed every run ends at empty, so the load run (stopped at 26%) showed a fake 189% error. Now it skips runs that stop early, with a test.

      Chapter 8
      
      The 30-second version
      
      
      I wanted to understand how a laptop's battery gauge behaves, so I built a small toolkit. A Python logger collects battery telemetry from Windows every 10 seconds. An analysis tool splits it into charge, discharge, and sleep sessions and runs automated checks, and a pytest suite runs in CI on every push. Across two laptops I found the OS time-remaining estimate off by up to 57%, the gauge frozen at 7% for half an hour before recalibrating, and an "overnight drain" that was really accidental wake-ups plus a never-sleep setting. I also caught and fixed bugs in my own logger and analysis.
      
      
      
      
      All numbers come from real logs collected Oct 11–12, 2026.
